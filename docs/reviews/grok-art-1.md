**Improve the proposal.** Do not drop the plates, and do not put them on the reading page. Covers can be Winter; the reading box must be the app’s own quiet, flat stage. Gemini’s “drop the plates” is right about the reading surface and wrong about the shelf.

**1. The split is right. Three constraints were missing.**

Plates as **covers only** (home 112px tile, bookshelf square) is the correct use: no decoding, square crop can keep one subject, softness is invisible at that size. Flat art on the **reading box** is the correct use: it must survive dimming, match the mascots, and not fight the letters.

Do not “pick B outright.” B wins only on the page the child reads. Winter on a cover is a real storybook at the moment the adult sits down; you do not get that from a fox that reads as a mounted head. Do not “pick A outright” either: three of five plates are portrait and go empty when forced into 350×250, they turn to mud after the bedtime filter, and one of them is a wolf killing a lamb.

What the proposal still has to lock:

- **[P1]** Compose every reading picture for **350×100 as well as 350×250**. The box shrinks on the child’s turn. A subject in the middle of a landscape (the exact failure of the Winter plates) will be clipped or squashed. Horizon, sky, ground, one prop. A banner, not a poster.
- **[P1]** Illustrate the **place**, not the target word. “Scene without figures” can still be a giant cheese sitting in the box while the child is about to decode *cheese*. That is the guessing path with extra steps.
- **[P1]** Drop the shepherd-boy plate. Do not crop it, tint it, or “use a gentler corner.” The subject of that plate is the attack. Cover that story with the same flat vocabulary as the non-Aesop books (hill, sheep as white ovals, a staff in the grass).
- **[P2]** Square-crop each remaining plate onto **one** subject with a two-value split (dark animal / light sky). Fox **or** crow, not both. Check the crop at 112px with the bedtime filter applied. If a plate becomes a brown square, it is not a cover.
- **[P2]** The five books with no plate, plus the shepherd boy, need covers that are **icons**, not landscapes: one object, filling most of the square, two or three colours, hard edges. A weak wide scene at 112px will look like the unfinished half of the shelf next to Winter.

---

**2. Guessing: the veil is real help. It does not erase the picture they just saw.**

A 4-year-old’s default in a picture book is: look at the interesting thing, say a word that fits, ignore the letters. Decoding is effortful; the picture is not. That is the behaviour this app is built to block.

The veil is the right tool for the **eyes**. For the second or two of the child’s word, the competing picture is gone and the prompt is “read the letters.” That stops the usual failure, which is gaze parked on the crow while the mouth invents a word.

It does **not** stop **memory**. The picture was just on screen while the adult read the lead-in. A 4-year-old holds a salient image easily. “The crow dropped the ___” after a plate of a crow holding cheese is a primed guess. Sentence context primes them too; you will never strip meaning out of a bedtime story. What you can do is make the picture a **weak** prime: a tree and a stump, not a labelled noun.

So: “detailed picture invites guessing” is **overstated as a reason to ban Winter from covers** (no decoding there). It is **understated** if the reading-page art is a literal depiction of tonight’s word, veil or not. Attention theft is the bigger 4-year-old problem anyway — Winter is more interesting than graphemes. Simple, calm, non-literal art on the page is doing pedagogical work, not just covering for weak figure drawing.

---

**3. Bedtime 11–25: a problem for identification, a mood for atmosphere, a bug if it looks like a missing image.**

After `saturate(0.6) brightness(0.5)`, plates sit in luminance 36–104 with contrast SD 11–25. At 2% brightness plus Night Shift that is a warm stain. Watercolour edges and ink lines are gone. You might keep a sky/ground split if the crop has one. You will not keep a mouse, a spear, or a crow.

- **Reading page:** a dim, soft picture at bedtime is desirable. You are putting a child to sleep, not running a gallery. But 11–25 is *so* gone that a 350×250 box reads as empty or broken — “is the picture loading?” That is why plates fail **on the page**, even before pedagogy. Flat art at 41–46 still reads as hills and sky. That is the right bedtime picture: present, quiet, not a comic.
- **Covers:** 11–25 **is** a problem if the child is meant to pick “the fox one” by sight. Five brown squares are not a bookshelf. The adult can read the title; the child cannot. **[P2]** Keep plate covers only where the square crop still has a clear dark/light mass under the filter; let titles carry the rest for the grown-up. Do not try to restore plate detail at 2% — fight the dimming and you fight bedtime.

Do not raise flat-art contrast to “fix” bedtime. Daylight 90–99 is already poster-hard. **[P2]** Use a quieter subset of the palette on the reading page so 41–46 after the filter is a dusk scene, not neon hills in a dark room.

---

**4. Mixed styles: not incoherent to a 4-year-old. Incoherent to an adult if half the covers are weak.**

A 4-year-old’s actual shelf is Disney, a board book, a library paperback, something drawn in crayon. They sort by “the fox one,” not by illustration school. “Some books look different from other books” **is** a bookshelf.

Gemini was reviewing a design system. The child is not.

The real risk is adult-side and real enough for a nightly app: four rich 1919 covers next to six timid SVG landscapes look like art that ran out. That is not a reason to drop Winter. It is a reason to make the drawn covers **bold and iconic**, and to let the Aesop plates be the “old books.” A 4-year-old will accept that immediately. **[P2]** Optional unifier: the same rounded square, the same cream rule, maybe a thin shared frame. Do not redraw Winter in flat style just so the shelf matches.

**5. Compositions for the five non-Aesop books (and every reading page, including Aesop).**

Do not take a fourth pass at faces. Treat “I cannot draw figures” as the style: **the people have left the stage.**

Rules that hold for all ten reading boxes:

- Wide banner: sky band, ground band, one prop on the horizon line.
- No faces, no full bodies, no three-quarter animal heads (that is how a fox becomes a mounted trophy and hair becomes a beard).
- If an animal must exist, it is a distant silhouette, a rear, or a part: tail over a log, ears in grass, a paw-print. At this size, sheep as white ovals already work — use that trick everywhere.
- The prop is **not** the decodable noun for that page.
- The 350×100 crop should still be a readable strip of sky-over-ground.

**Covers** (square, 112px): one object, cropped large.

Recipes, by story-type, so you are not inventing anatomy:

| Book kind | Reading banner (350×250 → 350×100) | Cover square |
|---|---|---|
| Panchatantra, river/crocodile type | River, far bank, a half-sunk log, fruit on the far shore. No animals. | One mango, huge. |
| Panchatantra, trap/net type | Grass, an empty net, morning light. | The net as a pattern filling the tile. |
| Panchatantra, cave/well/pot type | Cave mouth or well circle, one rope or stone. Dark interior, quiet. | The arch or the round well, two colours. |
| Original phonics, farm | Hills, sun, sheep as ovals. You already know this works. | One sheep-oval and a hill, or just the sun. |
| Original phonics, woods/path | Path, triangle trees, no walker. | One tree, cropped to trunk + crown. |
| Original phonics, home/bed | Window, lamp, **empty** bed. Correct for bedtime; no child to draw. | The lamp, or the window with dark blue. |

Aesop **reading** pages, same rule (plates stay on the cover only):

- Fox and crow: tall tree, stump, sky. No fox, no crow, no cheese if *cheese* is the word.
- Shepherd boy: hillside, oval flock, staff in the grass. No boy, no wolf, no spear.
- Lion and mouse: net on the ground, a large print, a small dark hole. No faces.
- Hare and tortoise: a dirt path, a rock, a distant post. Empty race.
- Ants and grasshopper: a mound of grain, dry grass, sun. Insects would be dots; skip them.

That set is consistent, calm, banner-safe, and it does not ask SVG to do portraiture.
